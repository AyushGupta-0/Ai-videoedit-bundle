# TEN DAYS — a 10-part survival series

**Format:** second person. The viewer *is* the survivor. REF A says "Meet Noor";
we say "You". It converts every survival fact into a decision the viewer is making,
which is what drives comments.

**Length:** 75–95s per reel. REF A runs 95s, so the ceiling is higher than our
explainer format. Then one 10-minute YouTube cut.

---

## THE SPINE — read this before any episode

On Day 1 you take a **hand-crank emergency radio** out of your car boot. It picks up
one message, looping: coordinates and an instruction to go south. Every episode you
hear a little more of it, and every episode it is the reason you survive.

**The twist, Day 10:** it was never a broadcast. The set is two-way. Every time you
cranked it to listen, you transmitted your position. The "loop" was someone answering
you — for ten days. You were never following the signal. It was following you.

This is why the series works: every episode plants the radio as the hero, and the
finale turns it into the threat. Rewatching Day 1 changes what Day 1 means.

**Second thread:** on Day 4 a car follows you and flashes `SOS` in headlights. You
drive away. On Day 7 something knocks on a sealed carriage door — three long, three
short, three long. Same signal. That is the person you abandoned. She is why you reach
the rig at all.

## EP01 · DAY 1 — THE TRAFFIC JAM
**Hook:** "If the outbreak catches you on a motorway, your car is the safest place
you will ever be. For about nine hours."

- `17:42` Traffic stops. Four lanes, nothing moving. Phones show no bars.
- People get out to look up the road. **Rule one: the people who get out are the
  first to go.** You stay.
- Doors locked, lights off, engine off — not for fuel, for *noise*. An idling engine
  is audible at 200 metres.
- Inventory card: half a tank · one bottle of water · a dead phone · and a hand-crank
  emergency radio that has been in the boot since your father put it there.
- `23:10` You crank it. A woman's voice, looping: *"…grid seven is gone… keep moving
  south…"*
- **Cliffhanger:** there's a second half to the loop. You crank again to hear it — and
  the handle snaps off in your hand.

**End card:** WOULD YOU HAVE GOT OUT OF THE CAR?

---

## EP02 · DAY 2 — FORTY MILES
**Hook:** "A car with no fuel is just an expensive coffin. You have forty miles left
and four hundred abandoned cars around you."

- The maths: 40 miles of range, 180 miles to the coast. You need roughly 20 litres.
- **How siphoning actually works:** gravity, not suction — the tank must sit above the
  can, and you never put your mouth on the tube. Any car after ~2005 has an anti-siphon
  valve in the filler neck, so you want old vehicles.
- Noise discipline: one triggered car alarm reaches everything within a mile. You
  disconnect each battery *before* you open anything.
- You get 11 litres out of a 1998 van. Card: `11 LITRES = 90 MILES`.
- **Cliffhanger:** as you finish, you see a child's car seat in the back of the van.
  Empty. And every door was locked from the inside.

**End card:** WHERE DID THE CHILD GO?

---

## EP03 · DAY 3 — THREE DAYS
**Hook:** "Three weeks without food. Three days without water. Today is day three."

- The service station is already stripped — because everyone takes the shelves.
  **Nobody takes the plumbing.**
- Where the water actually is: the toilet cistern (clean, 6 litres), the water heater
  tank (**40 litres**), the pipes themselves if you open the lowest tap in the building.
- You take a paper road map. Your phone has been dead for 36 hours and is never
  coming back.
- You crank the radio with a pair of pliers jammed in the broken handle. More of the
  loop: *"…six-two south…"*
- **Cliffhanger:** headlights in your mirror. Steady, 400 metres back. They have been
  there since the motorway.

**End card:** DO YOU STOP, OR DRIVE?

---

## EP04 · DAY 4 — THE CAR BEHIND YOU
**Hook:** "On day four you meet another survivor. Statistically, this is more dangerous
than meeting the dead."

- It follows for 60 miles and never closes the gap. You stop; it stops. You move;
  it moves.
- Your reasoning, shown: you have 90 miles of fuel and no weapon. You cannot defend a
  night stop. You will not lead a stranger to the only resources you have.
- You kill your lights and turn off at a junction.
- Behind you they flash their headlights. **Three long. Three short. Three long.**
- You keep driving. This is the decision the series is built on.
- **Cliffhanger:** in the mirror, their lights don't fade into the distance. They go
  *out*. And they do not come back on.

**End card:** WAS THAT THE WRONG CALL? *(it was — see Day 7)*

---

## EP05 · DAY 5 — THE END OF THE ROAD
**Hook:** "Your car is going to die. The only choice you get is whether you pick the
place."

- Every road south funnels into one bridge, and the bridge is a six-mile car park.
  Cars are a trap wherever roads narrow: bridges, tunnels, toll gates.
- You abandon it **on your terms**: pack built, water filled, map marked — not in a
  panic at 3am with nothing.
- You take the railway line instead. Why: elevated above the ground, fenced on both
  sides, dead straight, and completely empty — because everyone with a car took a road.
- Eleven miles of walking brings you to a freight yard. A locomotive is idling with
  no one in the cab.
- **Cliffhanger:** as you climb up, the radio loop finally plays its second half. It
  is not a rescue message. It is a warning.

**End card:** WHAT IS IT WARNING YOU ABOUT?

---

## EP06 · DAY 6 — TWENTY MILES AN HOUR
**Hook:** "A moving train is the safest vehicle on Earth right now. Nothing can board
it above twenty miles an hour."

- Why trains beat every other vehicle: ~1,000 miles of range on one tank, a steel box
  four feet off the ground, no traffic, and a route nobody else is using.
- The controls are simpler than a car: a throttle, a brake, and a **dead-man's switch**
  you have to press every 60 seconds or it stops the train.
- Which is why you sleep in 20-minute blocks, with your hand on it.
- The loop, finally whole: *"…do not approach the cities. Transit south. Rig Bravo.
  Six-two south."*
- Card: `RIG BRAVO — 62°S — 2,100 MILES`
- **Cliffhanger:** the train begins slowing. You have not touched the brake.

**End card:** SOMETHING PULLED THE EMERGENCY BRAKE.

---

## EP07 · DAY 7 — CARRIAGE 32
**Hook:** "A train only stops itself for two reasons. Something on the line ahead —
or someone in the carriages behind."

- The brake was pulled from the rear. To reset it you walk back **eleven carriages**,
  in the dark, on a stopped train.
- Rules you follow: one hand always free, torch pointed at the floor not ahead, and
  never open a door you cannot close behind you.
- Carriage 32's door is sealed from the inside.
- Then: knocking. In threes. **Three long. Three short. Three long.**
- **Cliffhanger:** it is the signal from Day 4. The person you drove away from has been
  following you for three days. Do you open it?

**End card:** WOULD YOU OPEN THE DOOR?

---

## EP08 · DAY 8 — SIX HUNDRED KILOS AN HOUR
**Hook:** "You open the door. Eight hours later that decision is the only reason you
are on a helicopter."

- She is a coastguard radio operator. She followed your transmissions — *she* is why
  she found you, which should worry you more than it does.
- She knows what Rig Bravo is, and she knows a decommissioned airfield with a fuelled
  Sea King on the apron.
- The brutal arithmetic: a Sea King burns **600 kg of fuel an hour**. Every kilo of
  cargo costs range, and range is the only thing keeping you alive.
- What you take: water, the map, the radio. What you leave: **all the food.** Food
  exists at the rig. Range does not.
- **Cliffhanger:** at 2,000 feet she says the thing you have been refusing to think
  about. *"We have fuel one way."*

**End card:** THERE IS NO COMING BACK.

---

## EP09 · DAY 9 — RIG BRAVO
**Hook:** "An offshore oil rig is the single most defensible structure on Earth in an
outbreak. Here is the arithmetic."

- **180 feet above the water.** One helideck, one boat landing, one ladder. They cannot
  swim, cannot climb, cannot survive the water temperature.
- It makes its own: desalination for unlimited fresh water, its own generators, 60
  bunks, and a year of stores for a crew of 40.
- Card: `RIG BRAVO — 60 BUNKS · 1 YEAR OF FOOD · 0 WAYS UP`
- But the rig is empty, and it has been running unmanned on auto for **40 days**.
- You find the crew log. They did not leave because of the infection. The last entry
  is four words long.
- **Cliffhanger:** the winter fuel isn't on the rig. It is **400 miles further south**,
  at a research station on the ice.

**End card:** WHAT DID THE LOG SAY?

---

## EP10 · DAY 10 — MINUS SIXTY
**Hook:** "If the outbreak catches you in Antarctica, you are the safest human alive.
Unless you have already made the mistake."

- The station: **-60°C**. They freeze solid in minutes. Cold is better than any wall,
  because cold never needs reinforcing.
- Stores: a year of frozen food, **10,000 gallons** of fuel, and every drop of water
  you will ever need, lying on the ground.
- You switch off the runway lights so nothing can find you from the air.
  `SHELTER ✓  FOOD ✓  COLD ✓`
- On paper you have thought of everything.
- **THE TWIST:** the radio is a two-way set. Every single time you cranked it to
  listen, you transmitted. The loop was never a broadcast — it was somebody answering.
  For ten days you have been telling someone exactly where you are. And you just told
  them where you'll be all winter.
- **Final line:** *"You were never following the signal. It was following you."*

**End card:** WHO WAS ON THE RADIO? · SEASON 2

## THE 10-MINUTE YOUTUBE CUT

Not the ten reels glued together — that reads as ten hooks in a row and people leave
at minute two. Same story, re-cut for a viewer who has already committed.

| Time | Section | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:40 | **Cold open** | The Day 10 twist, played without context: a hand cranking a radio, a voice answering, cut to black. Then: "To understand that, go back ten days." |
| 0:40–1:10 | **Premise** | The rules of this world, stated once: it has been 31 hours, the cities are gone, and you are in a car. |
| 1:10–3:40 | **Act I — The Road** (Days 1–5) | Reels 1–5, cliffhangers removed and replaced with cause-and-effect transitions. Expanded: the siphoning sequence in full, and the full map of why every road south funnels to one bridge. |
| 3:40–6:10 | **Act II — The Line** (Days 6–8) | Reels 6–8. Expanded: how a dead-man's switch works, the complete weight-vs-range table for the Sea King, and the Day 4 / Day 7 signal reveal given room to land. |
| 6:10–8:30 | **Act III — The Ice** (Days 9–10) | Reels 9–10. Expanded: the rig's full systems breakdown, and the crew log read out in full. |
| 8:30–9:30 | **The twist, properly** | Replay four earlier moments with the radio in frame, now that the viewer knows. This is the section that earns the rewatch, and it only works at long form. |
| 9:30–10:00 | **Resolution + hook** | The final line, the runway lights going out, and Season 2. |

**What the long cut adds that the reels cannot:** the survival mechanics in full
(siphoning, dead-man's switch, fuel-vs-range, desalination), the map as a continuous
object, and the twist-replay montage. Those four things are the reason to watch it
after seeing all ten reels.

**Production note:** build the reels first. The 10-minute cut is assembled from the
same plates, the same overlays and a re-recorded narration — no new art.
