# Hindi Voiceover — Recording Script & Performance Direction

Your voice, your delivery. This doc exists so I learn **how you speak** — where you push, where you
hold back, where you let silence do the work. Record this one, send it back, and I'll match every
future script to the rhythm you naturally fall into.

---

## 1. Setup (10 minutes, once)

| Thing | What to do |
|---|---|
| Mic | Phone earphones with inline mic are fine. Better: any USB mic. Don't use laptop built-in. |
| Distance | A fist's width from your mouth, slightly off to the side (stops the hard "p" pops). |
| Room | Small room, soft things around — bed, curtains, clothes. **Never** a bathroom or empty hall. |
| Time | Late night / early morning. Fans, traffic and fridge hum all land in the recording. |
| App | Voice Memos (iPhone) or any recorder set to **WAV / highest quality**. Not WhatsApp voice notes. |
| Level | Speak at your normal volume. If the meter touches the top, back off — clipping can't be fixed. |

**Record 5 seconds of pure silence before you start talking.** That lets me sample the room noise
and subtract it. This one trick does more for quality than an expensive mic.

**Do a full take, mistakes and all.** Don't stop and restart. If you fumble a line, pause two
seconds and say it again — I'll cut to the good one. Stopping/starting kills the energy.

---

## 2. How to read the direction

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **bold** | Push this word. Not louder — *leaner*. Slight weight, slight slow-down. |
| `…` | Real pause. Count one full beat in your head. Resist filling it. |
| `(↓)` | Drop your pitch. Let the line fall away instead of lifting at the end. |
| `(↑)` | Lift slightly — curiosity, a question hanging. |
| `[tone: x]` | The feeling underneath the line. |

The single biggest thing: **do not perform excitement.** This genre works when the narrator sounds
calm and slightly unsettled, like they're telling you something they only just found out. The facts
are dramatic; your voice doesn't need to be. Underplay it and the viewer leans in.

---

## 3. The script — "What if we could travel at the speed of light"

Target: ~57 seconds. Don't rush to hit it; I can adjust the visuals to your pace.

---

**[tone: casual, like you're starting a normal conversation]**

> Torch **on** karo.

`…`

> Us light ki speed hai… **teen lakh** kilometre per second. (↓)

**[tone: shift — now you're setting a challenge]**

> Ab usko **chase** karo.
> Aadhi light speed pe bhaago. Aur dobara naapo.

`…`

**[tone: this is the first "wait, what" moment — say it flat, let the fact do the work]**

> Woh abhi bhi aapse **utni hi** tezi se door ja rahi hai. (↓)
> Bilkul. Utni. Hi.

`…`

**[tone: matter-of-fact, almost a shrug]**

> Ye aapke **rocket** ki kami nahi hai.
> Ye **universe** ka rule hai. (↓)

`…`

**[tone: build — this is where the scale starts opening up]**

> Light speed tak pahunchne ke liye… aapko **infinite** energy chahiye.
> Duniya ka **har** engine… kaafi nahi hoga. (↓)

`…`

**[tone: lean in, quieter — the secret]**

> Lekin agar aap sirf **close** pahunch jao…
> **Ninety nine point nine** percent… (↑)

`…`

> …toh reality khud aapko **raasta** de deti hai.

**[tone: wonder — this is the beauty beat, slow down here]**

> Aage ke sitare ek **tunnel** mein simat jaate hain.
> Neeli **aag** ka tunnel. (↓)

`…`

> Space **khud** sikud jaata hai.
> Chaar light year ka safar… kuch **hafton** ka ban jaata hai.

`…`

**[tone: drop almost to a whisper on "dheere"]**

> Aur aapki **ghadi**… dheere ho jaati hai. (↓)

`…`

**[tone: pick the pace back up — we're taking a journey]**

> **Andromeda** chale jao.
> Aap wahan utróge… **atthais** saal baad.

`…`

**[tone: THE turn. This is the whole video. Say it slowly and let it sit.]**

> Lekin **Earth**… (↓)
> **Pachchees lakh** saal budhi ho chuki hogi.

`…`

**[tone: quiet, almost sad — don't push, just state it]**

> Jinko aap jaante the… **sab** ja chuke.
> Unke **shehar**… ja chuke. (↓)

`…`

**[tone: the resolve — calm, certain]**

> Light speed kabhi **speed limit** thi hi nahi.
> Ye future ka… **one-way darwaza** hai. (↓)

`…`

**[tone: the hook back to the start. Slight lift. Leave them hanging.]**

> Aur ye sab shuru hota hai…
> ek aisi cheez se… jo aap **roz** karte ho. (↑)

`…`

> Bina **soche**. (↓)

**[STOP. Do not add "follow", "subscribe", or anything else. The silence is the ending.]**

---

## 4. Why the ending matters

The reel **loops**. When it restarts, the viewer hears *"Torch on karo"* — which is literally the
answer to *"ek aisi cheez jo aap roz karte ho, bina soche."* The story only completes on the second
watch. That's what holds people for a repeat view, and repeat views are what the algorithm reads as
a strong signal.

So the last line must sound like a **question left open**, not a conclusion. If you end it with
finality, the loop breaks.

## 5. Three extra takes, please

After the main take, record these separately — 20 seconds total, and they teach me your range:

1. **Excited:** "Ye **bilkul** paagalpan hai." — your genuine enthusiasm voice
2. **Suspense:** "Aur phir… kuch aisa hua… jo kisi ne socha nahi tha." — your slow-burn voice
3. **Warm/kids:** "Chalo, ek kahani sunte hain." — soft, for the kids channel

With those I can write future scripts in the register that actually suits you, instead of guessing.
