# ANAYA — "Ten Days" · a 10-episode series

**Logline.** A six-year-old learns her father is coming home in ten days, and decides
to give him a welcome he will never forget. She has forty-two rupees.

**Why ten episodes.** One episode is one day of the countdown. The calendar is the
series clock, and it appears in every episode — the viewer always knows exactly how
long is left. That is what makes people come back: not curiosity about *what* happens,
but a deadline they can feel.

---

## The rule that shapes everything

Every shot is **Anaya alone**. This is a production constraint — the image model
cannot stage two characters — but it is also the show's voice, so we lean in:

- Other people exist **only in narration and in her reaction**. The shopkeeper, the
  farmer, her mother, her father: we hear about them, we watch her face receive them.
- This is how picture books work. A child watching has to *imagine* the other person,
  which is more involving than being shown one.
- Practically: never write a shot with two people in frame. Write her face instead.

**Her world** (reuse these settings so the series feels like one place):
the window · the calendar wall · the kitchen table · the front door · the rainy
window · the floor where she draws · the lane outside · the market · the field.

---

## The suspense rule

Every episode ends mid-breath, never on a resolution. The last line is a question
the next episode answers. Concretely, the last 3 seconds are always:

1. A beat where it looks like she has succeeded, **then**
2. One new fact that undoes it, **then**
3. Cut to black on her reaction — never on an explanation.

The caption under every post ends with **"Day N of 10."**

## Episode guide

### EP01 · "The Girl Who Waited" ✅ MADE
**Day 10 remaining.** Establishes the waiting: the window, the calendar, dinner alone,
asleep by the door, 211 days.
**New ending (re-cut):** the sound at the gate is not him — it is a letter.
*Papa is coming home. Ten days.*
**Cliffhanger:** she runs to her money tin. We do not see what is inside.
**Hook line:** "She had ten days. And forty-two rupees."

### EP02 · "Forty-Two Rupees"
**Day 9.** She empties the tin and counts, twice. Lists what a proper welcome needs:
firecrackers, his favourite lunch, a present. Forty-two rupees buys one of the three.
She writes a plan on the back of the letter.
**Cliffhanger:** she crosses out "present" — then stops, and un-crosses it.
**Why it works:** establishes the stakes as a number. Numbers make kids count along.

### EP03 · "The Firecracker Shop"
**Day 8.** The shop with the good firecrackers. She has been saving for the big box.
The shopkeeper tells her the price has gone up for the festival.
**Cliffhanger:** he says there is one box left at the old price — but it is promised
to someone else, who will come at six o'clock.
**Shot note:** her face in the shop doorway, lit by the display. No shopkeeper in frame.

### EP04 · "Six O'Clock"
**Day 7.** She waits outside the shop all afternoon to be there if the other buyer
does not come. She is still there at six.
**Cliffhanger:** footsteps behind her.
**Why it works:** a whole episode of pure waiting pays off the series' own theme.

### EP05 · "The Market"
**Day 6.** Fruits and vegetables for the lunch. She bargains, which she has never done.
The money runs out one item short — the one thing the dish cannot be made without.
**Cliffhanger:** she looks past the stalls, at the fields outside town.
**Shot note:** her hands counting coins; her face falling. Both single-character.

### EP06 · "The Farmer"
**Day 5.** She walks to the field and asks for one vegetable. The farmer says no —
not unkindly, but no. She walks home with an empty bag, and it begins to rain.
**Cliffhanger:** behind her, a voice calls her name.
**Why it works:** the first time an adult refuses her. Children feel this hard.

### EP07 · "What the Rain Took"
**Day 4.** The rain got into her school bag. The coloured paper she had been collecting
for the card is a pulp. She spreads it out to dry and it tears.
**Cliffhanger:** she remembers something — the art cupboard at school.
**Shot note:** the rainy window plate from EP01 returns. Reusing settings is the point.

### EP08 · "The Art Cupboard"
**Day 3.** Collecting scraps at school: gold foil, ribbon, one good sheet of card.
She builds the present card through the afternoon. It is better than she imagined.
**Cliffhanger:** she goes to write his name — and realises she does not know how to
spell it. She has only ever called him Papa.
**Why it works:** the smallest obstacle in the series and the most moving.

### EP09 · "The Night Before"
**Day 2.** Everything is ready and lined up by the door. She cannot sleep. A storm
comes in. She thinks about the train.
**Cliffhanger:** the phone rings at midnight.
**Shot note:** the asleep-by-the-door plate from EP01 returns, now with the storm.

### EP10 · "The Gate"
**Day 1 → 0.** Resolution. The welcome happens, imperfectly, which is why it lands:
one firecracker, a slightly burnt lunch, a card with his name spelled wrong.
**Final line:** "She spelled it wrong. He kept it for the rest of his life."
**Series hook:** the last shot is a new calendar going up on the wall — *he leaves
again in thirty days.* Season two.

## Pacing and format

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 42–55s. EP04 and EP09 can run short (35s) — tension likes brevity |
| Shots | 9–11 plates, 3.5–5s each |
| Voice | `af_sarah`, speed 0.95 — one narrator for the whole series |
| Captions | one plain white sentence, top third, matching the reference reels |
| Music | one bed for the whole series, two mixes: "longing" (EP01–07) and "hope" (EP08–10) |
| Camera | 18–38% travel per shot. Stills are the only motion; the camera never rests |
| Cover | the girl's face + "DAY N" + the episode title |

## Posting rhythm

Daily, same time. A countdown series only works if the audience can set a clock by it.
Post EP01 and EP02 on the same day to establish the hook, then one a day.

**Caption template:**
```
Day N of 10.

<one line of the episode's emotional fact>

<the cliffhanger as a question>
Follow so you don't miss Day N+1 ✦
```

## Why this series should work

It has the two things short-form serials need and most kids content lacks:
a **deadline** (ten days, on a visible calendar) and a **want** that a child
understands completely (make something nice for someone you miss). Every episode
is one attempt at that want, and every attempt fails in a new way until the last.
The obstacles escalate from money, to adults, to weather, to her own ability —
and the final obstacle is the smallest, which is what makes it land.
