# TEN DAYS — full narration scripts, EP01–EP10

Word-for-word. `[...]` are visual cues, not spoken. Each episode runs 60–75s at
~3.1s per line, which is REF C's measured pace.

## The formula (REF C, exactly)

1. `Imagine you're <role>, <place>, <distance> from anyone.`
2. Wrongness signal — one sense, not a statement
3. Mission + countdown clock
4. **Three rules.** Rule 3 is always the one that gets broken
5. **Good news / Bad news** — stores vs the single vulnerability
6. Timestamped approach
7. Escalation, in sound
8. **The voice** — uses your name, gives a plausible reason to open
9. **Contradiction** from a second source
10. **The tell** — it lies about something you can check
11. `Do you <action>?` → OPEN / HIDE
12. `Follow for Day N+1`

**The spine:** a hand-crank radio from your boot, Day 1. It is the second source in
every episode — the thing that catches the lie. On Day 10 you learn it transmits.
Ten days of listening was ten days of broadcasting your position.

## EP01 · DAY 1 — THE TRAFFIC JAM

> Imagine you're sitting in four lanes of traffic that haven't moved in forty minutes.
> Nobody's horn is working. That's the first thing you notice — nobody is honking.
> Then the radio station stops mid-sentence.
> `[hazard lights blink across a static line of cars]`
> Emergency broadcast, one loop: stay in your vehicle, do not walk the carriageway,
> recovery in ten days.
> **Rule one. Engine off.** An idling car is audible at two hundred metres.
> **Rule two. Hazards off.** Light is an address.
> **Rule three. If someone outside uses your name, do not answer.**
> Good news. Half a tank, a bottle of water, and a hand-crank radio your father left
> in the boot in 2009.
> Bad news. Your driver's window has been stuck two inches open since August.
> `[close on the gap. amber light through it]`
> **11:52 PM.** A torch beam starts moving down the line of cars. Slowly. Window to
> window.
> It stops at yours.
> Someone taps the glass. Twice.
> "Hey — it's Marcus, paramedic, I'm with the response unit. You're bleeding. Let me
> in and I'll patch you up."
> `[cut to the radio, CH 09]`
> Emergency broadcast: all response units were withdrawn six hours ago.
> And you're not bleeding.
> **Do you open the door?**

**End card:** OPEN / STAY · *Follow for Day 2*

## EP02 · DAY 2 — LEVEL FOUR

> Imagine you've driven your car to the fourth floor of a multi-storey car park,
> because concrete has no windows.
> You picked level four for a reason: too high to reach from the ground, low enough
> to drive down fast.
> The ramp below you is the only way up. One way in.
> Radio, same loop: recovery in nine days. Stay off the ground floor.
> **Rule one. Park facing the exit.** Always.
> **Rule two. Never sleep in the driver's seat** — you can't see behind you.
> **Rule three. Count the cars on your level before dark.**
> Good news. You counted. Eleven cars. Yours is the twelfth.
> Bad news. The stairwell door doesn't lock. It only closes.
> `[the door, a bar handle, no keyhole]`
> **3:40 AM.** Something pushes the stairwell door open and lets it close. Softly.
> Someone who did not want it to bang.
> Footsteps. Not shuffling — walking.
> "I know you're up here. I saw your brake lights. Which car are you in?"
> `[cut to your dashboard — dark]`
> You haven't touched the brakes since four o'clock yesterday.
> And when you look out across level four, you count again.
> **Thirteen cars.**
> **Do you start the engine?**

**End card:** DRIVE / WAIT · *Follow for Day 3*

## EP03 · DAY 3 — THE SERVICE STATION

> Imagine you're inside a motorway service station at two in the morning, and every
> shelf has already been taken.
> Three weeks without food. Three days without water. This is day three.
> But everyone loots the shelves. **Nobody loots the plumbing.**
> There are forty litres in the water heater and six clean litres in every cistern.
> Radio: recovery in eight days. Avoid enclosed retail.
> **Rule one. Fill from the lowest tap in the building** — gravity does the work.
> **Rule two. Never let a door close between you and your car.**
> **Rule three. If the lights come on, leave.**
> Good news. You get thirty-one litres and a paper road map.
> Bad news. The building has one exit, and you are at the back of it.
> `[floor plan: a long corridor, one door, you at the far end]`
> **2:06 AM.** Every light in the forecourt comes on at once.
> A voice over the station's own PA system — calm, bored, like a shift manager.
> "Store's reopening. Bring what you've got to the front and we'll check you out."
> `[your radio, CH 09]`
> Recovery broadcast: mains power to this grid failed on day one.
> Nothing here has electricity. And the lights are on.
> **Do you walk to the front?**

**End card:** WALK / HIDE · *Follow for Day 4*

---

## EP04 · DAY 4 — THE CAR BEHIND YOU

> Imagine you're doing fifty on an empty A-road and there have been headlights
> behind you for sixty miles.
> They never close. They never drop back. Exactly four hundred metres, for an hour.
> You slow down. They slow down. You speed up. So do they.
> Radio: recovery in seven days. Do not convoy with unverified vehicles.
> **Rule one. Never drive to where you sleep** — circle first.
> **Rule two. Never let a stranger see your fuel.**
> **Rule three. Do not stop on open road.**
> Good news. Ninety miles of fuel and a full tank of nerve.
> Bad news. There is one bridge south, and everybody has to use it.
> `[map: every road converging on one crossing]`
> **1:17 AM.** You kill your lights and take a junction without indicating.
> Behind you, they stop at the junction. And they flash their headlights.
> `[three long · three short · three long]`
> Three long. Three short. Three long.
> That's not a threat. That's **S.O.S.**
> And you keep driving.
> `[mirror: the lights go out — not fading. out.]`
> Thirty seconds later their lights don't fade into the distance. They go **out**.
> And they do not come back on.
> **Did you just leave someone to die?**

**End card:** YES / NO · *Follow for Day 5*

## EP05 · DAY 5 — THE SIGNAL BOX

> Imagine your car has eleven miles left and every road south ends at a six-mile
> queue for one bridge.
> So you stop choosing roads. You start walking the railway.
> Elevated. Fenced both sides. Dead straight. And completely empty — because
> everyone with a car took a road.
> Radio: recovery in six days. Transit south. Avoid all crossings.
> **Rule one. Walk the sleepers, not the ballast** — stone is loud.
> **Rule two. Count bridges, not miles.**
> **Rule three. Never shelter anywhere with one door.**
> Good news. Eleven miles in, there's a freight yard. And a locomotive, idling, no
> one in the cab.
> Bad news. Between you and it is a signal box. One room. **One door.**
> `[the box: raised, wooden, a single staircase]`
> **4:55 AM.** You go in to find the yard keys, and the staircase creaks behind you.
> "Driver? That you? I've been holding this train nine days, mate. Come down."
> `[radio, CH 09]`
> Recovery broadcast: all rail staff evacuated on day one.
> Then the loop runs past the point it has ever reached before. And the second half
> isn't a rescue message.
> It's a warning.
> `[static, then the first word of it]`
> **Do you go down the stairs?**

**End card:** DOWN / STAY · *Follow for Day 6*

## EP06 · DAY 6 — TWENTY MILES AN HOUR

> Imagine you're alone in the cab of a freight locomotive doing forty, and for the
> first time in six days, nothing can reach you.
> Nothing boards a train above twenty miles an hour. That's not bravado, it's physics.
> A thousand miles of range on one tank. Four feet of steel off the ground. No traffic.
> Radio: recovery in five days. Transit south. **Rig Bravo. Sixty-two south.**
> **Rule one. Hold the dead-man's switch** — release it for sixty seconds and the
> train stops itself.
> **Rule two. Sleep in twenty-minute blocks.** Never longer.
> **Rule three. Do not stop for anything on the line.**
> Good news. At this speed you reach the coast in two days.
> Bad news. You cannot stay awake for two days.
> `[the switch under a thumb · a timer counting 60 → 0]`
> **3:02 AM.** You wake up because the engine note has changed.
> Your thumb is still on the switch. You never let go.
> But the train is slowing.
> `[gauge: 40 → 31 → 22]`
> Somebody has pulled the emergency brake. From a carriage behind you.
> **Eleven carriages back.**
> **Do you go and look?**

**End card:** GO / RUN · *Follow for Day 7*

## EP07 · DAY 7 — CARRIAGE 32

> Imagine walking eleven carriages down a stopped freight train at four in the
> morning with one torch.
> The brake came off at carriage thirty-two. To start the train again, you have to
> reset it by hand.
> Radio: recovery in four days. Do not open sealed compartments.
> **Rule one. Torch at the floor, never ahead** — you see legs before you see faces.
> **Rule two. One hand always empty.**
> **Rule three. Never open a door you cannot close behind you.**
> Good news. Thirty-one carriages are empty. You check every one.
> Bad news. Carriage thirty-two is sealed. **From the inside.**
> `[a steel door, a bar across it on the far side]`
> **4:31 AM.** You put your hand on the brake lever, and the knocking starts.
> Not banging. Knocking.
> `[three long · three short · three long]`
> Three long. Three short. Three long.
> It's the car from Day Four. She's been following you for three days — on foot,
> after you left her.
> And she's on the other side of a door that was sealed **from the inside**.
> By her.
> **Do you open it?**

**End card:** OPEN / LEAVE · *Follow for Day 8*

---

## EP08 · DAY 8 — SIX HUNDRED KILOS AN HOUR

> Imagine you opened the door. And eight hours later that decision is the only
> reason you're in a helicopter.
> Her name is Priya. Coastguard radio operator, nine years. She found you because
> she tracked your transmissions.
> That should have worried you more than it did.
> Radio: recovery in three days. Rig Bravo. Sixty-two south.
> **Rule one. A Sea King burns six hundred kilos of fuel an hour.**
> **Rule two. Every kilo you load costs you range.**
> **Rule three. Range is the only thing keeping you alive.**
> Good news. The airfield's tanks are full, and she can fly it.
> Bad news. You have to choose what to leave on the ground.
> `[a scale: WATER 20kg · MAP 0.2kg · RADIO 3kg · FOOD 61kg]`
> You leave all of it. All sixty-one kilos of food.
> Because food exists at the rig. Range doesn't.
> **11:40 AM.** Two thousand feet over open water, she finally says it.
> "We've got fuel one way."
> Then she says the other thing.
> "Who have you been talking to on that radio?"
> `[you look at the crank handle. you have never spoken into it.]`
> **You've never spoken into it.**

**End card:** *Follow for Day 9*

---

## EP09 · DAY 9 — RIG BRAVO

> Imagine landing on an oil rig a hundred and eighty feet above the Southern Ocean,
> and realising it's the safest building on Earth.
> One helideck. One boat landing. One ladder. They can't swim, they can't climb, and
> they can't survive four degrees of water.
> It makes its own everything: desalinated water, its own power, sixty bunks, and a
> year of stores for a crew of forty.
> Radio: recovery in two days. Hold at Rig Bravo.
> **Rule one. Raise the boat-landing ladder.**
> **Rule two. Keep the helideck lights off.**
> **Rule three. Read the log before you touch anything.**
> Good news. Sixty bunks. A year of food. Zero ways up.
> Bad news. It's empty. And it's been running on auto for forty days.
> `[a corridor of open doors · meals still on a table]`
> **6:20 PM.** You find the crew log. Every entry is routine, for thirty-nine days.
> The fortieth is four words.
> `[handwriting: "IT CAME BY RADIO."]`
> **"It came by radio."**
> And then the thing that ends the night: the winter fuel isn't here.
> It's four hundred miles further south. On the ice.
> **Do you read the rest of the log?**

**End card:** READ / BURN IT · *Follow for Day 10*

---

## EP10 · DAY 10 — MINUS SIXTY

> Imagine you're standing in a research station in Antarctica, and you are,
> genuinely, the safest person alive.
> Minus sixty outside. They freeze solid in under two minutes. Cold is better than
> any wall, because cold never needs repairing.
> A year of frozen food. Ten thousand gallons of fuel. And every litre of water
> you'll ever need, lying on the ground outside.
> **Rule one. Shelter.** `[✓]`
> **Rule two. Food.** `[✓]`
> **Rule three. Cold.** `[✓]`
> You switch off the runway lights so nothing can find you from the air.
> On paper, you have thought of everything.
> `[the checklist, all three ticked, holding]`
> Then Priya picks up your radio and turns it over.
> And she asks why a receiver has an aerial.
> `[close: a transmit switch. worn. used.]`
> It's a two-way set.
> Every single time you cranked it to listen — Day One in the traffic. Day Three in
> the service station. Day Five in the signal box —
> you were **transmitting**.
> The loop was never a broadcast.
> It was someone answering you.
> For ten days you have been telling something exactly where you are.
> And four hours ago, you told it where you'll be all winter.
> `[outside: the runway lights come back on. by themselves.]`
> **You were never following the signal. It was following you.**

**End card:** WHO WAS ON THE RADIO? · *Season 2*

## Running the series

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 60–75s. EP10 can run 90s — the twist needs air |
| HUD | `●DAY N/10` + live clock + `RECOVERY IN N DAYS`, top-left, every frame |
| Captions | white caps, bottom, **one word amber** per line |
| Rule cards | red `RULE #N` badge over a full-frame statement |
| End card | the question + two buttons, always |
| Voice | one narrator, close-mic, flat and factual. The content is dramatic; the read must not be |

**Why the rules matter structurally:** they are not decoration. Stating three rules
tells the audience what breaking one looks like *before* it happens, so when the
voice outside uses your name, they feel it land without being told. Rule 3 is always
the one that gets broken.

**Why the radio works:** in every episode it is the thing that catches the lie, so
the audience learns to trust it completely. That is the entire setup for Day 10.
